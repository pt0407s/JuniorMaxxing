## Topic 1: The Periodic Table

### Key points
- Periodic table organizes elements by **properties** and predicts **chemical behavior**
- **Essential biological elements:** C, H, O, N, P, S — these form the backbone of macromolecules
- **Periodic trends**, especially **electronegativity**, influence bond formation and molecule stability

### You need to know
- **Electronegativity** = an atom's ability to attract electrons in a bond
- Electronegativity **increases** going **right** across a period and **up** a group
- Fluorine (F) is the most electronegative element
- The 6 essential biological elements: **C, H, O, N, P, S**
  - C, H, O, N → in all macromolecules
  - P → in nucleic acids (DNA/RNA) and ATP
  - S → in proteins (disulfide bonds)

## Topic 2: The Electron

### Key points
- **Electrons** determine how atoms form bonds and establish molecular structure
- **Valence electrons** are responsible for how atoms interact and bond
- **Electron configurations** affect the formation of **ions**, crucial for electrolyte balance in biological systems

### You need to know
- **Valence electrons** = electrons in the outermost shell. These are the ones that participate in bonding.
- Atoms want a **full outer shell** (octet = 8, or 2 for H/He)
- **Ions** form when atoms gain or lose electrons to achieve a full shell:
  - **Cation** = loses electrons → positive charge (metals, left side)
  - **Anion** = gains electrons → negative charge (nonmetals, right side)
- Example: Na (1 valence e⁻) → loses 1 → Na⁺. Cl (7 valence e⁻) → gains 1 → Cl⁻
- Ions like Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻ are **electrolytes** — they regulate nerve function, muscle contraction, osmotic balance

## Topic 3: Redox Reactions

### Key points
- Essential for how cells **capture, store, and release energy** — cellular respiration and photosynthesis
- **Hydrogen atoms:** gain = **reduction**, loss = **oxidation**
- **Oxygen atoms:** gain = **oxidation**, loss = **reduction**
- Electrons in **high-energy molecules** (like glucose) are transferred through **carriers** (NAD⁺/NADH, FAD/FADH₂)
- Electrons move toward **lower-energy states**, releasing usable energy to power cell work

### You need to know — the mnemonic that saves you

**OIL RIG:** **O**xidation **I**s **L**oss, **R**eduction **I**s **G**ain (of electrons)

- **Oxidation** = losing electrons (or losing H, or gaining O)
- **Reduction** = gaining electrons (or gaining H, or losing O)
- It's always a paired reaction — one thing gets oxidized, another gets reduced (LEO says GER)
- **Cellular respiration:** glucose is **oxidized** (loses H/e⁻), oxygen is **reduced** (gains H/e⁻ → water)
- **Photosynthesis:** water is **oxidized** (loses e⁻), CO₂ is **reduced** (gains e⁻ → glucose)
- **Electron carriers:**
  - NAD⁺ → NADH (gains electrons = **reduced**)
  - NADH → NAD⁺ (loses electrons = **oxidized**)
  - FAD → FADH₂ (reduced) / FADH₂ → FAD (oxidized)
- When NADH donates electrons to the electron transport chain, energy is released to make ATP

## Topic 4: Types of Chemical Bonds

### Key points
- **Ionic, covalent, and metallic bonds** differ in strength and behavior
- **Bond energy** plays a role in metabolism, especially **ATP breakdown**
- **Electronegativity** determines whether covalent bonds are **polar or nonpolar**
- **Covalent bonds** form stable structures like DNA and proteins
- **Hydrogen bonds** (weaker) are vital for DNA structure and protein folding
- **Ionic compounds** (salts) regulate osmotic balance and nerve function

### You need to know — the three bond types

| Bond type | How it forms | Strength | Example |
|-----------|-------------|----------|---------|
| **Ionic** | Transfer of electrons (metal + nonmetal) | Strong in solid, breaks in water | NaCl |
| **Covalent** | Sharing of electrons (two nonmetals) | Strong | DNA, proteins, water |
| **Hydrogen** | Attraction between H and electronegative atom (O, N, F) | Weak (individually) | DNA base pairs, water |
| **Metallic** | Sea of shared electrons among metal atoms | Varies | Not biologically relevant |

**Key distinctions:**
- **Covalent** can be **polar** (unequal sharing) or **nonpolar** (equal sharing)
- **Hydrogen bonds** are NOT covalent bonds. They're intermolecular attractions. Many together = strong collective effect (DNA double helix).
- **Bond energy** = energy required to break a bond. Breaking ATP's bonds **releases** energy the cell uses.
- ATP → ADP + Pᵢ releases energy. This is bond energy driving metabolism.

## Topic 5: Polar vs Nonpolar Molecules

### Key points
- **Electronegativity differences** between atoms determine molecular polarity
- **Polar molecules** (like water) dissolve other polar substances; nonpolar don't
- **"Like dissolves like"** — solubility based on polarity
- Polarity influences **membrane structure** — hydrophobic and hydrophilic regions determine permeability
- **Polar molecules form hydrogen bonds**
- Polarity affects **enzyme binding** and **cell signaling**

### You need to know

**Polar** = unequal sharing of electrons → partial charges (δ+, δ−)
- Happens when atoms have **different electronegativities**
- Example: H₂O — O is more electronegative, pulls electrons toward itself, so O is δ− and H is δ+

**Nonpolar** = equal sharing of electrons → no partial charges
- Happens when atoms have **similar electronegativities** (like C–H) or when dipoles cancel (like CO₂, CH₄)
- Example: oil, methane, hydrocarbon chains

**"Like dissolves like":**
- Polar dissolves polar (salt dissolves in water ✓)
- Nonpolar dissolves nonpolar (oil dissolves in gasoline ✓)
- Polar does NOT dissolve nonpolar (oil and water don't mix ✗)

**In biology:**
- Cell membranes: **phospholipid bilayer** — hydrophilic (polar) heads face water, hydrophobic (nonpolar) tails face inward
- This is why membranes are **selectively permeable** — nonpolar molecules pass through, polar molecules need transport proteins
- Enzymes: active site shape + polarity must match substrate for binding

## Topic 6: Water

### Key points
- Water's **polarity** and ability to form **hydrogen bonds** make it essential for life

### You need to know — water's properties (all stem from polarity + H-bonds)

1. **Cohesion** — water molecules stick to each other (H-bonds) → surface tension, water transport in plants
2. **Adhesion** — water sticks to other surfaces → capillary action
3. **High specific heat** — H-bonds absorb/release lots of energy → temperature regulation (why sweating cools you)
4. **Universal solvent** — dissolves polar substances (because water is polar, "like dissolves like")
5. **Less dense as ice** — H-bond lattice spreads molecules out → ice floats, life survives under ice
6. **Cohesion + adhesion** = capillary action = water moves up trees against gravity
