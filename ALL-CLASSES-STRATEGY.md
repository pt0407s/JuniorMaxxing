## Block schedule advantage

You only have 4 classes per day. That means:
- **Only 4 classes worth of homework per night** — not 8
- **Each class meets every other day** — you get a built-in extra day for homework/study between class sessions
- **Use the off-day wisely** — the night after a class, do that class's homework while it's fresh. The night before the next session, pre-read for it.

### The block schedule nightly loop

**A Day night (prep for B Day + process A Day):**
- Process today's A Day classes (notes → Anki): ~20 min
- Do A Day homework: ~45-60 min
- Pre-read for tomorrow's B Day classes: ~15 min
- Anki reviews (all subjects): ~15 min

**B Day night (prep for A Day + process B Day):**
- Process today's B Day classes (notes → Anki): ~20 min
- Do B Day homework: ~45-60 min
- Pre-read for tomorrow's A Day classes: ~15 min
- Anki reviews (all subjects): ~15 min

**Total: ~1.5-2 hr/night** — sustainable, leaves room for clubs and free time

## The 6-week cycle across all 8 classes

### Week 1-2: Foundation
- Get all 8 syllabi, log grading categories
- Set up grade tracker for all 8 classes
- Start Anki for all content-heavy classes (Bio, Chem, APUSH, Physics, Precalc)
- Introduce yourself to every teacher — one sentence after class
- Figure out each teacher's test/quiz/lab style

### Week 3-4: First tests hit
- Major category tests likely in APs
- Apply the 3-day test prep protocol (recall sheet → practice → self-quiz)
- Lab reports likely due in Chem/Bio/Physics
- Start lab reports the day of the lab

### Week 5-6: Second tests + grade check
- More tests + labs
- **Check your grade in ALL 8 classes** — know where you stand before the period closes
- If any class is below 97, find out if there's extra credit or makeup BEFORE it's too late
- Bank your Minor category 100s — never lose a free point

## The reality check

8 classes (6 APs + 2 electives) on block schedule is a heavy but manageable load.

**The math:**
- ~2-2.5 hr/night study × 5 nights = 10-12.5 hr/week
- + 2-3 hr weekend = 12-15.5 hr/week total
- This leaves room for clubs, free time, and sleep IF you're efficient
- If you're regularly exceeding 3 hr/night, something is wrong — check for:
  - Low-utility techniques (re-reading, highlighting)
  - Distraction (phone nearby)
  - Not processing during class (doing after what you should do during)
  - Procrastination on homework (doing it at the last minute = slower)

**The edge:** In a competitive school where everyone is grinding, the students who get 100s aren't the ones who study the most — they're the ones who study the smartest. Active recall, spaced repetition, interleaving, and processing during class are what separate 100 from 95 when everyone is working hard.
