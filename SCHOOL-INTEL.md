## Critical implication: NO prior students to ask

Legacy Ranch opened in August 2024. You are in the **first cohort of juniors** at this school. There is nobody who took AP Bio at Legacy Ranch before you. The "ask a student who had this teacher last year" advice **does not apply here** — there is no "last year" for AP courses at this school.

### What this means for your strategy:
1. **You can't rely on past intel about teacher test styles** — you have to figure it out in real-time during the first 2 weeks
2. **The teachers may also be teaching AP Bio for the first time at this school** — they're figuring it out too
3. **The AP program is being built from scratch** — AP Human Geography was just added for 2025-26, and AP Precalc replaced OnRamps Precalc. The 2026-27 year is likely the first time many AP courses are offered at LRHS
4. **This is actually an advantage** — new AP teachers often follow the CED closely (less likely to go rogue), use AP Classroom resources heavily, and may be more generous with grading as they establish the program

## What to do instead of asking past students

Since there's no prior cohort, you need to gather intel FAST in the first 2 weeks:

### Week 1: Observe and document
- **Which bio teacher do you have?** Cannon or Maddox? Their style matters.
- **What textbook/platform?** You already know it's Pearson (from your study guide)
- **Test format:** First quiz or test will reveal everything — note the format, question style, source of questions
- **Does the teacher use AP Classroom?** If yes, Progress Checks are likely part of your grade
- **Lab frequency:** How often? What format do they want?
- **Vocab quizzes:** Confirmed yes (your study doc mentions them) — these are Minor category free points
- **"Proof of preparation" grade:** Confirmed yes — show your notes for a grade. Always do this.

### Week 2: Ask the teacher directly
- "What do your tests usually look like?" (they may have taught elsewhere before)
- "Should I focus more on the textbook or the slides for tests?"
- "How are labs graded — is there a rubric you can share?"
- "What's the best way to prepare for your quizzes?"

Teachers at a new school are usually MORE willing to share this info because they want students to succeed and build the program's reputation.

## The new-school advantage

Being at a brand-new school with a new AP program has real benefits:
1. **Teachers are motivated** — they want the AP program to succeed and grow
2. **Small class sizes** — 10.5:1 ratio means more individual attention
3. **No entrenched grade curves** — less likely to have "the teacher never gives above a 95" culture
4. **AP Classroom is likely heavily used** — new AP teachers lean on College Board resources
5. **You can help set the culture** — being a top student in a new program gets you noticed
