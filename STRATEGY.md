## What the research actually says

### 1. Most students use the WRONG techniques (Dunlosky et al. 2013, meta-analysis 2021 — 242 studies, 169,179 participants)

The two highest-utility study techniques, backed by the most evidence:
- **Practice testing (active recall)** — self-quizzing, flashcards, practice problems
- **Distributed practice (spaced repetition)** — spreading review over time, not cramming

The five LOWEST-utility techniques — the ones most students default to:
- Highlighting/underlining
- Re-reading
- Summarization
- Keyword mnemonics
- Imagery use

**Takeaway:** If your studying looks like "re-read notes + highlight textbook," you're using the 5 worst techniques. Switch to active recall + spaced repetition and you'll learn more in less time.

### 2. Good technique compensates for less time (Theobald 2025, British Journal of Educational Psychology)

When students planned better, monitored their understanding, and concentrated, they needed LESS time to reach the same achievement level. On days students studied many hours with low concentration, negative affect (stress, frustration) was highest. On days they studied less but procrastinated less, negative affect was lowest.

**Takeaway:** Efficiency > hours. A focused 90-minute session beats a distracted 3-hour session. And it feels better. This is how you protect free time.

### 3. Spaced repetition has a massive effect size (meta-analysis, 21,415 learners)

Spaced repetition beat standard studying with a standardized mean difference of 0.78. That's large. In practical terms: students using spaced repetition scored roughly 3/4 of a standard deviation higher.

**Takeaway:** Anki (or similar) daily, even 10-15 min, is one of the highest-ROI things you can do. It turns passive "review" into active recall + automatic spacing.

### 4. Interleaving dramatically improves math/science performance (Rohrer 2020, RCT with 54 classes)

7th graders who did interleaved math practice (mixed problem types) scored 61% vs 38% on a delayed test (effect size d=0.83). That's huge. Interleaving forces you to CHOOSE the right strategy, not just execute it.

A separate study (Sana & Yan 2022) found interleaved quizzes in high school science classes beat blocked quizzes (63% vs 54%).

**Takeaway:** For Precalc, Chem, Bio, Physics — don't do 20 of the same problem type. Mix problem types within a study session. It feels harder but learns better.

### 5. Top students study LESS, not more (Cal Newport's research on high-performers)

Patterns observed in top students:
- Study in **short focused chunks** (morning/afternoon), not long unbroken stretches
- Study in **isolation** — not groups, not dorms, not busy public spaces
- **Process information during class**, not after — take smart notes, ask questions, stay 5 min after to clean up notes
- **Never "study" vaguely** — always a concrete plan with specific actions
- "Stealth studying" — slice prep into 5-10 min chunks integrated into the day (walking, between classes)

**Takeaway:** The goal isn't more hours at your desk. It's capturing and processing info as it comes, so test prep is review, not learning from scratch.

### 6. Distraction kills performance (CBE Life Sciences Education study)

Students reported being distracted ~20% of study time, and distraction negatively predicted exam performance — even after controlling for total study time and academic preparation.

**Takeaway:** Phone in another room while studying. This is a free performance boost with zero extra effort.

### 7. Extracurriculars HELP, not hurt (PLOS One, 2016; multiple studies)

Participation in organized activities is associated with higher school engagement, lower stress, and better academic achievement. Students in multiple activities concurrently had the BEST performance. The caveat: motivation matters — self-determined motivation for activities transfers to academic motivation.

**Takeaway:** Clubs aren't the enemy of grades. They're correlated with better grades. Keep them. The enemy is wasted study time and poor technique.

## The system: built for your constraints

### Core principle: Capture-Process-Review, not Cram

| Phase | When | What | Time |
|-------|------|------|------|
| **Capture** | In class | Smart notes (Cornell or structured), ask questions, stay 5 min after to clean up | 0 extra min |
| **Process** | Same day, 10-15 min per class | Turn notes into Anki cards + fill gaps | ~1.5 hr total |
| **Review** | Daily, ongoing | Anki reviews (auto-scheduled) + interleaved practice | 20-30 min |
| **Test prep** | 3-5 days before test | Practice tests + interleaved problems + FRQs | 1-2 hr/session |

This front-loads the work. By test time, you're reviewing, not learning.

### The daily engine (total ~2-2.5 hr/night, not 5+)

1. **Anki reviews (20-30 min)** — all subjects combined. Do this first, every day, no exceptions. This is your spaced repetition engine. Phone off.
2. **Same-day processing (10-15 min per class that had new content)** — convert class notes into Anki cards. If you didn't understand something, flag it and look it up or ask next class.
3. **Homework/assignments (remaining time)** — do these with phone in another room. Focused. Fast.
4. **Pre-read for tomorrow (10 min per class, optional but high-ROI)** — skim the next topic so class = review.

### The weekly structure

| Day | Focus |
|-----|-------|
| Mon-Fri | Daily engine (Anki + processing + HW). ~2-2.5 hr/night |
| Sat | 1 practice test or FRQ set for whichever subject has a test next week. Interleaved problem set for math/science. ~2 hr |
| Sun | Plan the week. Review Anki flags. Make sure no assignment is missing. ~1 hr |

### Test prep protocol (starts 5 days out)

- **Day -5:** Make a 1-page active recall sheet (questions, not answers) from notes
- **Day -4:** Take a practice test or do released FRQs. Grade it.
- **Day -3:** Do interleaved practice problems (mix types). Focus on what you got wrong.
- **Day -2:** Re-do the practice test or FRQs. Fix remaining gaps.
- **Day -1:** Light Anki review + skim recall sheet. Sleep 7+ hrs.
- **Day 0:** Skim recall sheet morning of. Done.

### The 100 math (for all classes — 60/40 split)

- Major (60%): Need 100 avg. Zero margin for error. Every test, every lab, every project = 100.
- Minor (40%): Need 100. Never miss HW, never skip a quiz prep, always show notes proof.
- Overall: 0.6(100) + 0.4(100) = 100. This is the only formula that works.
- A single 98 on a test: 0.6(98) + 0.4(100) = 98.8 — 100 is gone for that period.

The minor category is fully in your control. Never lose a free point there. See [GRADING.md](GRADING.md) for the full breakdown.

## What to STOP doing

- Re-reading notes (low utility)
- Highlighting textbooks (low utility)
- Long unbroken study sessions (causes fatigue, lower efficiency)
- Studying with phone nearby (distraction = -performance)
- "Studying" without a specific goal (vague = wasted time)
- Cramming the night before (massed practice < spaced practice)
- Doing 20 of the same problem type in a row (blocked practice < interleaved)

## What to START doing

- Anki daily, all subjects, 20-30 min total
- Interleaved problem sets for math/science
- Process notes the SAME day, not before the test
- Phone in another room during study sessions
- Concrete daily study plan (specific actions, not "study bio")
- Practice tests/FRQs 4-5 days before every test
- Go to office hours within first 2 weeks + after any bad test

## Time budget reality check

Assuming school ~8am-3pm, clubs ~3-5pm some days, you have evenings.
- Anki: 25 min
- Note processing: ~60 min (4 classes with new content × 15 min)
- Homework: ~45-60 min
- Total: ~2-2.5 hr/night, 5 nights/week = 10-12.5 hr/week
- Weekend: ~3 hr
- **Total: ~13-15 hr/week of focused study**

This is sustainable. It leaves evenings mostly free. The research says this beats 25 hr/week of distracted, low-technique studying.

If you find yourself needing more than 2.5 hr/night regularly, something is wrong — you're either using low-utility techniques, not processing during class, or getting distracted.
