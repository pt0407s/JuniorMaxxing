# AP English Language & Composition (AP Lang)

## Overview
> **Study plan:** [STUDY-PLAN.md](STUDY-PLAN.md) | **Resources:** [../RESOURCES.md](../RESOURCES.md) | **Grading:** [../GRADING.md](../GRADING.md)
Skills-based course, not content-based. No memorization. Tests rhetorical analysis, argumentation, and synthesis writing.

## Grading
Major 60% (essays, tests, projects) / Minor 40% (HW, classwork, quizzes, participation)

## Class strategy
- **No content to memorize** — it's all skills. Practice > studying.
- **Ask your teacher early:** "What do you look for in a top-scoring essay?" Every English teacher has different priorities. Do exactly what they want.
- **Annotate everything** you read — for rhetorical choices (ethos/pathos/logos, diction, syntax, tone, structure)
- **Participate in every discussion** — often 10-15% of grade, most students leave it on the table
- **Revise when allowed** — if essays can be revised for a higher grade, ALWAYS do it. Free points.
- **Build a bank of ~15 flexible examples** (historical events, books, current events) you can adapt to any argument prompt
- **Read op-eds daily** (NYT, Atlantic) — 10 min — to absorb style and build evidence bank

## Three essay types
1. **Rhetorical Analysis** — analyze how an author builds argument (rhetorical triangle, appeals, strategies)
2. **Argument** — defend, challenge, or qualify a claim using your own evidence
3. **Synthesis** — use provided sources to build an argument (like a DBQ but for English)

## Key resources
- AP Classroom AP Daily videos
- Past AP Lang FRQs (released on AP Central)
- Your teacher's feedback is the #1 resource — go to them with drafts

## 6-week approach
- English grades are the most subjective — build the teacher relationship early
- **Write 1 practice essay per week** minimum — timed (40 min)
- **Go to office hours with your essays** — ask specifically what would make it a 9/9
- **Read every text assigned** — falling behind on reading kills discussion grades and essay content
