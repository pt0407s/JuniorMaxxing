## This class is different

AP Lang is **skills-based, not content-based.** There's nothing to memorize. You're being graded on your ability to:
1. Analyze rhetoric (how authors build arguments)
2. Build arguments (using evidence and reasoning)
3. Synthesize sources (use provided sources to build an argument)

This means: **practice > studying.** You can't cram for an essay.

## The three essay types

| Type | What it tests | Time | Mean (2025) |
|------|--------------|------|-------------|
| **Rhetorical Analysis (FRQ 2)** | How an author builds argument | 40 min | 3.30/6 |
| **Synthesis (FRQ 1)** | Use 3+ sources to build argument | 15 min read + 40 min write | — |
| **Argument (FRQ 3)** | Build argument on given topic | 40 min | — |

**Key insight:** Rhetorical Analysis mean is 3.30/6 — most students summarize instead of analyze. If you can write genuine ANALYSIS (not summary), you're already above average.

## Annotation system

When reading any text for class, annotate for:
- **Rhetorical choices:** diction, syntax, tone, structure, imagery, figurative language
- **Appeals:** ethos (credibility), pathos (emotion), logos (logic)
- **Purpose:** what is the author trying to accomplish?
- **Audience:** who is the author writing to?
- **Strategies:** how does the author build the argument?

This is practice for FRQ 2 (Rhetorical Analysis). Every text you read is a potential analysis prompt.

## Test prep protocol (starts 5 days before test)

See [test-prep-checklist.md](../test-prep-checklist.md) for the full checklist.

### Lang-specific test prep:
- **Day -5:** Review rhetorical terms. Read 2-3 past FRQ prompts + scoring guidelines.
- **Day -4:** Write 1 practice essay (timed). Type it. Don't look anything up.
- **Day -3:** Read student samples on AP Central — compare yours to a 6 vs a 3.
- **Day -2:** Review evidence bank. Practice thesis statements for 5 different prompts.
- **Day -1:** Light review. Read 1 op-ed for style. Sleep 8 hours.

### On the test:
- **Read the prompt twice** — make sure you understand what's being asked
- **Write a clear thesis FIRST** — everything follows from the thesis
- **Rhetorical Analysis:** COMMENTARY, not summary. "The author uses metaphor" = summary. "The author uses metaphor to make X tangible, making the reader feel Y" = analysis.
- **Synthesis:** Use at least 3 sources. Don't just quote — explain how they support YOUR argument.
- **Argument:** Defensible thesis + specific evidence. Vague examples lose points.
- **Manage time:** 40 min per essay. Don't spend 25 min on the first and rush the rest.

## 6-week approach
- English grades are the most **subjective** — build the teacher relationship early
- **Ask your teacher early:** "What do you look for in a top-scoring essay?" Do exactly what they want.
- **Write 1 practice essay per week** minimum — timed (40 min)
- **Go to office hours with your essays** — ask specifically what would make it a 9/9
- **Read every text assigned** — falling behind on reading kills discussion grades and essay content
- **Participate in every discussion** — often 10-15% of grade, most students leave it on the table
- **Revise when allowed** — if essays can be revised for a higher grade, ALWAYS do it. Free points.
