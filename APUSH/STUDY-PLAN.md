## DBQ/LEQ/SAQ practice

### DBQ (Document-Based Question) — 25% of AP exam
- **Rubric:** Thesis (1pt), Contextualization (1pt), Evidence from docs (2-3pt), Sourcing/HIPP (2pt), Complexity (1pt)
- **Practice thesis statements constantly** — DBQ and LEQ live or die on the thesis
- **HIPP:** Historical context, Intended audience, Purpose, Point of view — know this cold
- **Use ALL documents** — if there are 7 docs, reference all 7

### LEQ (Long Essay Question) — 15% of AP exam
- Same rubric as DBQ minus documents
- Need a defensible thesis + specific, accurate evidence
- Build a bank of ~15 flexible examples you can adapt to any prompt

### SAQ (Short Answer Questions) — 20% of AP exam
- **Easy points live here** — practice the format
- Each SAQ has 3 parts (a, b, c) — answer all three
- Be concise. 2-3 sentences per part. Don't overthink.

## Test prep protocol (starts 5 days before test)

See [test-prep-checklist.md](../test-prep-checklist.md) for the full checklist.

### APUSH-specific test prep:
- **Day -5:** Make a 1-page timeline of the periods being tested
- **Day -4:** Do practice MCQs (source-based — APUSH MCQs use primary/secondary sources)
- **Day -3:** Write 1 practice DBQ or LEQ — time yourself (60 min for DBQ, 40 for LEQ)
- **Day -2:** Review Anki for all key terms/dates/people in the tested periods. Practice SAQs.
- **Day -1:** Review timeline. Re-read thesis statements from practice. Sleep 8 hours.

### On the test:
- **MCQ:** Read the source first, then the question. The source IS the question.
- **SAQ:** Answer all 3 parts. Be concise. 2-3 sentences.
- **DBQ/LEQ:** Write a defensible thesis FIRST. Then evidence. Then sourcing (DBQ).
- **Manage time:** Don't spend 20 minutes on MCQ #1. Keep moving.

## 6-week approach
- Each 6-week period covers ~2-3 historical periods
- **Biggest risk:** Falling behind on reading. One missed chapter compounds. Never skip the reading.
- **Pre-read the textbook section before class** — if you fall behind on reading, you can't catch up
- **Take Cornell-style notes** in class — questions on left, notes on right, summary at bottom
- **Practice thesis statements constantly** — DBQ and LEQ live or die on the thesis
- APUSH is about ARGUMENT, not memorization. Practice writing defensible claims with evidence.
