How to Study  
AP Biology is a college level course, and you will be required to study the material outside of class. The time we spend in class together will be used to go over topic basics, practice the material presented in the textbook and apply our understanding of science concepts. There will not be time during lecture to cover all of the material required for you to do well on exams and the AP test. Therefore, you will need to spend some time each night reading from the textbook, studying your notes, watching review videos, and completing assignments on AP Classroom. You do not have to do all of these activities\! I urge you to try each of the activities and find what works best for you. This is a skill that will help you maximize your time and learning in this class and in classes you take in the future. 

I recommend you choose one option from each column. However, you are welcome to do all four if you want more practice and in depth understanding of the material. You will be required to show proof of your preparation (copy of your notes) for a grade.

| Ineffective Studying | Effective Studying |
| :---- | :---- |
| Passive | Active |
| “Going over” or “reading over” the notes | Explaining the notes aloud in your own words |
| Using only one modality of learning, probably visual | Visiting the content in as many ways as possible-- visually, auditorily, conversationally, kinesthetically |
| Studying the same way for every type of assessment | Thinking about the type of assessment you are taking and adjusting your studying accordingly |
| Looking over content without intentionally trying to commit it to memory | Generating mnemonics (songs, rhymes, acronyms, silly or unusual ways to remember) |
| Studying all the notes or content in their entirety | Targeting the information you don’t know or know the least |
| Letting the notes speak for themselves | Creating questions and answering or discussing them |
| Cramming or having a marathon studying session | Revisiting material over time in spaced intervals |
| Only trying to learn the facts or wording from your notes | Coming up with examples and making the learning relevant to your experience |
| Leaving the information in its original form or order | Categorizing, grouping, manipulating, dissecting or reorganizing information |
| Assuming the information is stored in your brain without checking to make sure | Practicing retrieving content in the notes from your memory or self-quizzing by covering up the notes and reciting them |
| Treating every bit of information as if it has the same importance | Prioritizing content by separating main ideas from smaller details |
| Treating all content equally | Diagnosing what you understand versus what you don’t understand and figuring out how to understand the things you don’t understand |
| Reading through the notes in your head without ever attempting to explain them yourself | Explaining the content aloud to yourself or another person |
| Not asking for help | Asking a friend or teacher to explain things that are unclear |
| Regurgitating the material in your notes without thinking about what it means | Concentrating on the meaning of your notes |
| Storing the content temporarily in your short-term memory | Focusing on the long-term, thinking about how you can understand the content beyond the day of the test |
